# Imperator — Phase 0 design proposal

Stage 3 of the workflow: the stack, the architecture, the screens, the look,
and the build plan, for the author's approval before any game code.

Inputs: `SPEC.md` (the game), `KILLGATE.md` (what the numbers showed),
`FRAME-BATTLE.md` (large battles), `ECONOMY.md` (money), `PROVINCES.md` (the
map and order of battle).

---

## 1. Decisions already made

| Decision | Source |
|---|---|
| Turn-based, monthly turns, March 68 start, about 40 turns to a win | SPEC §3 |
| Four acts; four historical claimants reach the last; you are the fifth | SPEC §4, §8 |
| Full empire, about 37 provinces + 4 Italy regions | SPEC §7, PROVINCES |
| Buy / negotiate / fight / bypass encounters; towns, raiders, brigands | SPEC §6 |
| One win (hold Rome); four roads; seven background choices | SPEC §12–13 |
| Legionary / Centurion / Legate at about 35 / 15 / 5% | SPEC §14, KILLGATE |
| Holding Rome is its own game | SPEC §13a |
| **Palette: scarlet, gold, purple, grey, silver, crimson; light grey to calm busy screens** | Author, 2026-10-24 |
| **Large battles on an animated pixel field, controlled by order buttons** | Author, 2026-10-24; FRAME-BATTLE |
| **Province management in the manner of Crusader Kings / Total War** | Author, 2026-10-24; §6 below |
| Phone first, installable, offline, no accounts | All previous builds |

## 2. Stack

| Choice | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | **Vanilla ES modules, no build step, bundled to one file for release** (shape B: Centennial Farm, The Lists, Touchline) | A simulation game. The kill-gate model is already this shape and runs headless in Node for bots and measurement |
| Rendering | DOM for screens; **canvas** for the empire map and the battlefield | Pixel art at integer scale; many moving units |
| Map data | Built once from Natural Earth coastlines and rivers by a committed tool (as Vikingr's `build-map.ts`); provinces assigned to tiles from their capitals, borders hand-corrected | Real coastlines; no hand-drawn map to maintain |
| Saves | `localStorage` (guarded), versioned key, migrations, text export `IMPR1.` | Every past build's contract |
| Tests | Node test runner; invariants, bots, measurement, phone smoke (Playwright, preinstalled) | Same as Touchline and The Lists |
| Deploy | GitHub Pages from the default branch; itch.io zip | Same as before |
| Dependencies | None at runtime | — |

## 3. Architecture

```
src/
  data/        logic-free tables: provinces, edges, legions, buildings,
               people (historical figures), backgrounds, difficulty, economy
  engine/      pure: (state, input) -> state. No DOM, no Date, no Math.random
    rng.js         named streams, state in the save
    state.js       newGame(opts), the turn pipeline (written order, one record out)
    derive.js      every number the UI shows: odds, costs, lock reasons
    ledger.js      the only place money moves; purse == ledger
    world.js       provinces, buildings, taxes, levies, unrest, frontiers
    legions.js     loyalty, pay, debts, mutiny
    encounter.js   buy / negotiate / fight / bypass: ONE resolver
    skirmish.js    small fights (2–3 choices)
    battle.js      the field: ONE resolver for hand play, auto and measurement
    rivals.js      AI factions, on the player's functions
    history.js     the timeline and its triggers; the anchoring rule
    rome.js        recognition, the hold, conspiracies, generals
    save.js        versioned saves, migrations, IMPR1 codes
  ui/          reads view(state), computes nothing
    map.js  province.js  army.js  politics.js  rome.js  encounter.js
    skirmish.js  field.js (battle canvas)  dispatches.js  legate.js  newgame.js
  art/         sprite sheets generated by committed code
tools/         bot, measure-*, check, smoke, build-map, make-sprites, bundle
```

Import direction: `ui → engine → data`. The engine never imports the UI.

**The kill-gate sim retires.** Its model moves into `src/engine/` in Phase 1,
and the measurement tools call the engine itself, so the numbers can never
drift from the game (The Company's lesson).

## 4. Screens

| Screen | What it does |
|---|---|
| **New game** | Difficulty, start province, then the seven background choices (or a quick-start legate for each road) |
| **The empire** (main) | The map. Provinces coloured by holder, armies as standards with a route line, frontier threat as a glow. Tap a province or an army. A top bar with the month, your treasury, legitimacy and promises outstanding |
| **Province** | Crusader Kings / Total War style; see §6 |
| **Army** | Its legions: men, loyalty, pay, promises owed, experience. Set a route. March past (bypass) or stop at each body |
| **Encounter** | One body at a time: buy, negotiate, fight, bypass, each showing what it costs and what it needs, never what it will do |
| **Skirmish** | Small fights: 2–3 choices (approach, commit, pursue) and a short report. Under a minute |
| **The field** | Large battles; see `FRAME-BATTLE.md` |
| **Politics** | Rivals (with their generals' characters), letters, the Senate, your friends and enemies, the promises ledger |
| **Rome** | From Act IV: grain, the crowd, the Praetorians, recognition and the **whispers meter** (§7) |
| **Dispatches** | The month's news, dated by when it left (news lag), rumour marked as rumour |
| **The legate** | Your stats, background, friends and enemies, and your road so far |

**Monthly flow.**
1. Read the dispatches.
2. Act: provinces, armies, politics. Defaults carry forward, so quiet months
   take a few taps.
3. **End the month.**
4. Encounters and battles stop and ask as armies meet.
5. Read the report.

## 5. Visual identity

### Palette

The author's six colours, with light greys to calm busy screens:

| Token | Colour | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--purple` | Imperial purple | `#4E1A45` | Headers, the emperor, Rome, the Senate |
| `--purple-2` | Lighter purple | `#7A3770` | Hover and selected states, Act IV accents |
| `--scarlet` | Scarlet | `#C8102E` | **You**: your provinces, your cohorts, primary buttons |
| `--crimson` | Crimson | `#8C1C2B` | Danger, losses, enemies at the gates, routing |
| `--gold` | Gold | `#C9A227` | Rules, laurels, money, highlights; never body text on light |
| `--gold-2` | Pale gold | `#E8D48A` | Gold on dark grounds; selected chips |
| `--silver` | Silver | `#B9BDC3` | Neutral provinces (the Senate's), borders, disabled |
| `--silver-2` | Bright silver | `#DADDE1` | Panels on grey |
| `--grey-9` | Iron | `#2B2826` | Body text |
| `--grey-6` | Stone | `#6C6762` | Secondary text |
| `--grey-1` | Marble | `#ECEAE6` | Main background: the light grey that calms |
| `--grey-0` | White marble | `#F6F4F0` | Cards |

Rules:
- Light marble grounds; colour is for meaning, not decoration.
- At most one strong colour field per screen (usually the purple header).
- Scarlet always means you. Crimson always means danger. Gold always means
  money or honour.

**Rivals need their own colours** on the map and the field. Six colours cannot
separate eight factions, so rivals get muted earth hues that sit under the
main palette:

| Faction | Colour |
|---|---|
| Galba | Ochre |
| Otho | Slate blue |
| Vitellius | Olive |
| Vespasian | Bronze |
| Minor rivals | Taupe, with a pattern |
| The Senate | Silver |

Shields on the field use the same colours, since both armies are Roman.

### Type
A carved-capital display face for titles (Cinzel or similar, from Google
Fonts) and a plain readable face for everything else. Numbers in tabular
figures.

### Pixel art
- **The empire map:** 1 tile ≈ 25 km, about 240×130 tiles, drawn at integer
  scale with pan and zoom. Sea in grey-blue, land in pale stone, provinces
  tinted by holder.
- **The field:** about 96×64 tiles at 4×. Earth, grass, vineyard rows, roads,
  rivers, town walls. Cohorts as small blocks in shield colours, eagle standards
  in gold.
- **Sprites generated by committed code,** never only the output: blocks,
  standards, cavalry, pila streaks, buildings icons. Same pipeline as Silk Road
  and Touchline.
- **Scene art for big moments** (proclamation, entering Rome, the Senate,
  death) can come later from supplied sources, quantised to the palette.

A style tile with the palette and mock screens is in `docs/style-tile.html`.

## 6. Province management

Each province screen has four tabs. Defaults carry forward, and an **empire
list** lets you change many provinces at once (set every quiet province to
normal tax, for example), so twenty provinces never mean twenty screens a
month.

| Tab | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | Holder, governor, revenue, grain, unrest, loyalty to you, frontier threat. **Taxes** in four steps: light, normal, heavy, extortion. Each shows its revenue and its monthly unrest change. **Grain:** ship to Rome, hold, embargo |
| **Buildings** | Slots by tier: legion province 4, grain 3, interior 3, procurator 2, Italy 4. One build at a time per province. Each shows cost, months, upkeep and effect (`ECONOMY.md`) |
| **Forces** | Legions and auxiliaries stationed here, with loyalty. **Station a garrison:** leave a legion or auxiliaries. **Raise forces:** auxiliary cohorts (fast) or a legion (months). Recruits come from this province and carry its group (for appeals to comrades) |
| **Governor** | Appoint from your friends and clients. Each has a trait (honest, greedy, able, timid) that changes revenue, unrest or loyalty. A capable man might not stay loyal |

**Pushback, recorded:** Crusader Kings and Total War campaigns last centuries
or decades; this one lasts three or four years. Buildings are therefore works
a governor could finish in a season (roads, granaries, harbours, a stone camp,
a mint), not monuments, and each must pay back within about a year of play or
it will never be built. Every building is a new system, so the measurement
tools must show each one being worth building somewhere, or it is cut.

## 7. Fixes for the kill gate's open issues

| # | Issue | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Military weakest on easier levels | The field gives the military road its edge: ruses, better generalship, absorbing broken legions. Re-measure after Phase 2 |
| 2 | Too few small fights off the military road | New skirmish types: river crossings, ambushes in the passes, rival detachments harassing a march, a town's sally, deserters. All fight-or-avoid |
| 3 | Conspiracy the top killer | **The whispers meter** on the Rome screen and top bar in Act IV, fed by legitimacy and Rome's unrest. An informer or a friend warns you a month ahead when a plot forms, and each road has a counter: purge (military), the Senate (politics), largesse (charisma), informers (wealth) |
| 4 | 14–19% stalemates | Rival AI: claimants who hold Rome send a second army out to break the weakest rival in reach; claimants who cannot take Rome make terms (fold into a stronger one) instead of waiting |
| 5 | Bypass decides nothing | Becomes **March past**, a route setting on the army: faster, but every body left behind cuts supply. Paired with a time pressure the bot and player feel: a rival marching on Rome shows its arrival month on the map |
| 6 | Politics background strong everywhere | Watch at playtest |

## 8. Saves

- Key: `imperator.save` (a contract, never renamed).
- Payload: `{ v, state }`, with a migration chain and fixtures from every
  version.
- Text export `IMPR1.` (deflated JSON, base64url) and a file download.
- Autosave at the start of every month; one manual slot.

## 9. Instruments

| Tool | Question | Must assert |
|---|---|---|
| `check` | Is anything impossible happening? | Kill-gate invariants, plus purse == ledger, and no building or order the UI offers that the engine ignores |
| `bot` | Can a full campaign be played? | Every run advances months and ends; no stalls |
| `measure:campaign` | Are the win rates still on target? | The kill-gate matrix, with the noise floor |
| `measure:battle` | Is the field tactical, not arithmetic? | Hand vs auto vs random targets; no pure-order policy wins; relief matters |
| `measure:options` | Is every encounter option and building worth using somewhere? | Ablations; an option or building that changes nothing gets cut |
| `smoke` | Does it work on a phone? | 390×844: every screen renders, no overflow, no raw tokens, reload keeps the save |

## 10. Build plan

| Phase | Builds | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Engine and instruments** | Port the model to `src/engine`; ledger; saves; bot; `check`; `measure:campaign`; the map built from Natural Earth; PWA shell; plain placeholder screens for map, province, army | Bot plays full campaigns; win rates match the kill gate within noise; installs on a phone |
| **2. Calibration** | Three pieces at full polish: the **province screen** (with buildings), the **encounter card**, and **one battle on the field** (Second Bedriacum) | Author's verdict on look, feel, number of orders and density |
| **3. Vertical slice** | Act I and Act II from Germania Superior, through to proclamation: about 10 months of play | The author wants to play the next month |
| **4. Breadth** | All starts, Acts III–IV, all rivals, Rome, the whispers meter, all buildings, all skirmish types | Every measurement on target; every start and road playable |
| **5. Release** | itch zip, store art, page copy, Pages | Played from the uploaded zip |

## 11. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Scope: a map game, a management game and a tactical game at once | The calibration phase settles one piece of each before the rest are built |
| The field could be tedious or solved | Measured targets set before tuning (`FRAME-BATTLE.md`); orders capped at six per sector; one-tap rounds |
| 41 regions on a 390-pixel screen | Pan and zoom; an empire list as the alternative to the map; big tap targets on armies |
| Buildings as decoration | Each must measure as worth building somewhere, or it is cut |
| Province management becoming chores | Defaults carry forward; batch changes from the empire list; at most a few decisions in a quiet month |

## 13. Phase 1, as built (2026-10-24)

Where this section and the plan above differ, this section wins.

**What exists**
- `src/engine/`: the kill-gate model split into 15 modules, plus diplomacy
  (`FRAME-DIPLOMACY.md`), the player's actions, `view()`, versioned saves and a
  money ledger.
- `src/data/map.js`: a 204×165 tile map built from Natural Earth by
  `tools/build-map.js`.
- `src/ui/`: the phone shell. It has a map with pan and zoom, plus province,
  provinces, army, diplomacy, Rome, news and legate screens. It also has
  encounter and offer cards, new game (difficulty, start, quick start or seven
  life choices, name), an end screen, and save codes (`IMPR1.`).
- PWA manifest, service worker, generated icons.

**Verified**
- **The split is faithful.** With diplomacy off and the old settings, the
  engine matches `sim/model.js` on 252 campaigns.
- **`tools/check.js`** checks its rules every turn over 168 campaigns,
  including purse = start + ledger.
- **`tools/bot.js`** plays campaigns through `act()` and `view()` only, with no
  stall or rejected action. A save reloaded at month 12 plays on identically.
- **`tools/smoke.mjs`** at 390×844: every tab renders with no overflow, raw
  tokens or console errors; 24 months played; end screen; save survives a
  reload.

**Found and fixed while building**
- In Act I rivals treated you as an open enemy, and an idle player lost his
  legion by month 10 in 84% of games. Now your legion counts as the Senate's
  until you take a province or are proclaimed. Beaten in Act I, it falls back to
  a neighbouring province instead of being destroyed.
- Dispatches were trace lines ("F11 rises at NIT"). Now they are written news,
  dated, and late from far provinces.

**Measured with diplomacy** (`tools/measure-campaign.js`, 140 runs per road per level)

| | Wealth | Military | Politics | Charisma | All | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Legionary | 44% | 29% | 54% | 45% | 43% | 35% |
| Centurion | 24% | 11% | 33% | 22% | 22% | 15% |
| Legate | 15% | 5% | 10% | 12% | 11% | 5% |

- **Diplomacy makes the game easier.** Its biggest lever, submission, was
  reined in (an office owed, trust first, one demand every three months).
- **Win rates are about 1.5× target.** Retuning waits for Phase 2, because
  buildings and the field will move these numbers again.
- **Military is the weakest road**, as at the kill gate.

**Known gaps carried to Phase 2**
- Buildings, governors and the tactical field.
- The whispers meter is shown but plots give no month's warning yet.
- Map borders need two hand corrections.
- Fonts load from Google, so the first offline load uses fallback faces.

## 14. Phase 2 (calibration), as built (2026-10-24)

Three pieces at full polish for the author's verdict, plus the fixes promised
in §7.

**1. The field** (`src/engine/battle.js`, `src/ui/field.js`, `docs/FRAME-BATTLE.md`)
- **One resolver** for every large battle: hand play, "leave it to the
  legates", AI against AI, and measurement.
- **Orders:** three sectors with six orders each, plus the reserve, cavalry on
  both wings, and the legate (ride, rally, appeal across the lines, ruse).
- **Line relief rotates lines.** The tired line passes back through the gaps
  and rests.
- **A spent line cannot brace**, and a broken front passes its panic to the
  line behind.
- **Four enemy generals**, who answer repetition. Their intent is never shown.
- **The screen is pixel art:** cohorts as blocks of soldier dots that thin as
  men fall, volleys, dust, relief and rout animated, night falling.
- **A calibration jump** on the title screen opens straight into Second
  Bedriacum.

Measured (`tools/measure-battle.js`, 300 battles a cell):

| Policy | Even 4 v 4 | Outnumbered 4 v 6 | Outnumbering 6 v 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Careful hand play | 58% | 26% | 74% |
| Leave it to the legates | 45% | 31% | 73% |
| Never relieve | 37% | 30% | 71% |
| Always hold | 19% | 13% | 74% |
| Always charge | 10% | 7% | 66% |
| Random | 5% | 3% | 94% |

**Open:**
- About a third of even battles end in a draw at night.
- Random orders do oddly well with superior numbers.

**2. Provinces** (`src/data/buildings.js`, `src/engine/buildings.js`)
- **Eight works**, each with a cost, months and upkeep.
- **Governors:** three named candidates per province with a trait; sometimes a
  friend from your past.
- **The sheet has four tabs:** overview (taxes, grain, games), buildings,
  forces and governor.
- **Every work matters somewhere.** `tools/measure-buildings.js` forbids each
  in turn: every one is built, and each changes 3–22 of 24 campaigns. The arms
  workshop was decoration and was folded into the levy office and the stone
  camp.

**3. The encounter card:** an emblem, the facts, and a line on what each
option risks.

**Fixes from §7 and from playing it**
- **Plot warnings.** Plots form a month before they strike. You may be warned;
  informers paid in time may uncover them. Conspiracy fell from 20–25% of
  endings to 3–4%.
- **Act I dead end.** A usurper could take your province while you plotted,
  leaving nothing to approach. Fixed, and the interface bot now fails if Act I
  ever has no way forward.
- **Map:** south-east Spain, La Mancha and the Balearics belong to
  Tarraconensis; the strip east of the Rhine mouth is outside the empire.
- **Snowball.** The player held 18 legions when Act IV opened. Now an army of
  more than eight legions in one place loses men each month (supply), and fewer
  beaten legions change sides.

**Balance** (`tools/measure-campaign.js`, 140 runs per road per level)

| | Wealth | Military | Politics | Charisma | All | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Legionary | 41% | 20% | 51% | 36% | 37% | 35% |
| Centurion | 19% | 6% | 21% | 13% | 15% | 15% |
| Legate | 4.3% | 3.6% | 2.1% | 8.6% | 4.6% | 5% |

- Every start on Legate wins 1.3–7.5%.
- **Open:**
  - Military still trails on the easier levels.
  - Politics is below and charisma above the 3–8% band on Legate.
  - Dying with your army is the top cause of loss (27–30%).
