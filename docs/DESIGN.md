# Imperator — Phase 0 design proposal

Stage 3 of the workflow: the stack, the architecture, the screens, the look,
and the build plan, for the author's approval before any game code.

Inputs: `SPEC.md` (the game), `KILLGATE.md` (what the numbers showed),
`FRAME-BATTLE.md` (large battles), `ECONOMY.md` (money), `PROVINCES.md` (the
map and order of battle).

---

## 1. Decisions already made

| Decision | Source |
|---|---|
| Turn-based, monthly turns, March 68 start, about 40 turns to a win | SPEC §3 |
| Four acts; four historical claimants reach the last; you are the fifth | SPEC §4, §8 |
| Full empire, about 37 provinces + 4 Italy regions | SPEC §7, PROVINCES |
| Buy / negotiate / fight / bypass encounters; towns, raiders, brigands | SPEC §6 |
| One win (hold Rome); four roads; seven background choices | SPEC §12–13 |
| Legionary / Centurion / Legate at about 35 / 15 / 5% | SPEC §14, KILLGATE |
| Holding Rome is its own game | SPEC §13a |
| **Palette: scarlet, gold, purple, grey, silver, crimson; light grey to calm busy screens** | Author, 2026-10-24 |
| **Large battles on an animated pixel field, controlled by order buttons** | Author, 2026-10-24; FRAME-BATTLE |
| **Province management in the manner of Crusader Kings / Total War** | Author, 2026-10-24; §6 below |
| Phone first, installable, offline, no accounts | All previous builds |

## 2. Stack

| Choice | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | **Vanilla ES modules, no build step, bundled to one file for release** (shape B: Centennial Farm, The Lists, Touchline) | A simulation game. The kill-gate model is already this shape and runs headless in Node for bots and measurement |
| Rendering | DOM for screens; **canvas** for the empire map and the battlefield | Pixel art at integer scale; many moving units |
| Map data | Built once from Natural Earth coastlines and rivers by a committed tool (as Vikingr's `build-map.ts`); provinces assigned to tiles from their capitals, borders hand-corrected | Real coastlines; no hand-drawn map to maintain |
| Saves | `localStorage` (guarded), versioned key, migrations, text export `IMPR1.` | Every past build's contract |
| Tests | Node test runner; invariants, bots, measurement, phone smoke (Playwright, preinstalled) | Same as Touchline and The Lists |
| Deploy | GitHub Pages from the default branch; itch.io zip | Same as before |
| Dependencies | None at runtime | — |

## 3. Architecture

```
src/
  data/        logic-free tables: provinces, edges, legions, buildings,
               people (historical figures), backgrounds, difficulty, economy
  engine/      pure: (state, input) -> state. No DOM, no Date, no Math.random
    rng.js         named streams, state in the save
    state.js       newGame(opts), the turn pipeline (written order, one record out)
    derive.js      every number the UI shows: odds, costs, lock reasons
    ledger.js      the only place money moves; purse == ledger
    world.js       provinces, buildings, taxes, levies, unrest, frontiers
    legions.js     loyalty, pay, debts, mutiny
    encounter.js   buy / negotiate / fight / bypass: ONE resolver
    skirmish.js    small fights (2–3 choices)
    battle.js      the field: ONE resolver for hand play, auto and measurement
    rivals.js      AI factions, on the player's functions
    history.js     the timeline and its triggers; the anchoring rule
    rome.js        recognition, the hold, conspiracies, generals
    save.js        versioned saves, migrations, IMPR1 codes
  ui/          reads view(state), computes nothing
    map.js  province.js  army.js  politics.js  rome.js  encounter.js
    skirmish.js  field.js (battle canvas)  dispatches.js  legate.js  newgame.js
  art/         sprite sheets generated by committed code
tools/         bot, measure-*, check, smoke, build-map, make-sprites, bundle
```

Import direction: `ui → engine → data`. The engine never imports the UI.

**The kill-gate sim retires.** Its model moves into `src/engine/` in Phase 1,
and the measurement tools call the engine itself, so the numbers can never
drift from the game (The Company's lesson).

## 4. Screens

| Screen | What it does |
|---|---|
| **New game** | Difficulty, start province, then the seven background choices (or a quick-start legate for each road) |
| **The empire** (main) | The map. Provinces coloured by holder, armies as standards with a route line, frontier threat as a glow. Tap a province or an army. A top bar with the month, your treasury, legitimacy and promises outstanding |
| **Province** | Crusader Kings / Total War style; see §6 |
| **Army** | Its legions: men, loyalty, pay, promises owed, experience. Set a route. March past (bypass) or stop at each body |
| **Encounter** | One body at a time: buy, negotiate, fight, bypass, each showing what it costs and what it needs, never what it will do |
| **Skirmish** | Small fights: 2–3 choices (approach, commit, pursue) and a short report. Under a minute |
| **The field** | Large battles; see `FRAME-BATTLE.md` |
| **Politics** | Rivals (with their generals' characters), letters, the Senate, your friends and enemies, the promises ledger |
| **Rome** | From Act IV: grain, the crowd, the Praetorians, recognition and the **whispers meter** (§7) |
| **Dispatches** | The month's news, dated by when it left (news lag), rumour marked as rumour |
| **The legate** | Your stats, background, friends and enemies, and your road so far |

**Monthly flow.**
1. Read the dispatches.
2. Act: provinces, armies, politics. Defaults carry forward, so quiet months
   take a few taps.
3. **End the month.**
4. Encounters and battles stop and ask as armies meet.
5. Read the report.

## 5. Visual identity

### Palette

The author's six colours, with light greys to calm busy screens:

| Token | Colour | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--purple` | Imperial purple | `#4E1A45` | Headers, the emperor, Rome, the Senate |
| `--purple-2` | Lighter purple | `#7A3770` | Hover and selected states, Act IV accents |
| `--scarlet` | Scarlet | `#C8102E` | **You**: your provinces, your cohorts, primary buttons |
| `--crimson` | Crimson | `#8C1C2B` | Danger, losses, enemies at the gates, routing |
| `--gold` | Gold | `#C9A227` | Rules, laurels, money, highlights; never body text on light |
| `--gold-2` | Pale gold | `#E8D48A` | Gold on dark grounds; selected chips |
| `--silver` | Silver | `#B9BDC3` | Neutral provinces (the Senate's), borders, disabled |
| `--silver-2` | Bright silver | `#DADDE1` | Panels on grey |
| `--grey-9` | Iron | `#2B2826` | Body text |
| `--grey-6` | Stone | `#6C6762` | Secondary text |
| `--grey-1` | Marble | `#ECEAE6` | Main background: the light grey that calms |
| `--grey-0` | White marble | `#F6F4F0` | Cards |

Rules:
- Light marble grounds; colour is for meaning, not decoration.
- At most one strong colour field per screen (usually the purple header).
- Scarlet always means you. Crimson always means danger. Gold always means
  money or honour.

**Rivals need their own colours** on the map and the field. Six colours cannot
separate eight factions, so rivals get muted earth hues that sit under the
main palette:

| Faction | Colour |
|---|---|
| Galba | Ochre |
| Otho | Slate blue |
| Vitellius | Olive |
| Vespasian | Bronze |
| Minor rivals | Taupe, with a pattern |
| The Senate | Silver |

Shields on the field use the same colours, since both armies are Roman.

### Type
A carved-capital display face for titles (Cinzel or similar, from Google
Fonts) and a plain readable face for everything else. Numbers in tabular
figures.

### Pixel art
- **The empire map:** 1 tile ≈ 25 km, about 240×130 tiles, drawn at integer
  scale with pan and zoom. Sea in grey-blue, land in pale stone, provinces
  tinted by holder.
- **The field:** about 96×64 tiles at 4×. Earth, grass, vineyard rows, roads,
  rivers, town walls. Cohorts as small blocks in shield colours, eagle standards
  in gold.
- **Sprites generated by committed code,** never only the output: blocks,
  standards, cavalry, pila streaks, buildings icons. Same pipeline as Silk Road
  and Touchline.
- **Scene art for big moments** (proclamation, entering Rome, the Senate,
  death) can come later from supplied sources, quantised to the palette.

A style tile with the palette and mock screens is in `docs/style-tile.html`.

## 6. Province management

Each province screen has four tabs. Defaults carry forward, and an **empire
list** lets you change many provinces at once (set every quiet province to
normal tax, for example), so twenty provinces never mean twenty screens a
month.

| Tab | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | Holder, governor, revenue, grain, unrest, loyalty to you, frontier threat. **Taxes** in four steps: light, normal, heavy, extortion. Each shows its revenue and its monthly unrest change. **Grain:** ship to Rome, hold, embargo |
| **Buildings** | Slots by tier: legion province 4, grain 3, interior 3, procurator 2, Italy 4. One build at a time per province. Each shows cost, months, upkeep and effect (`ECONOMY.md`) |
| **Forces** | Legions and auxiliaries stationed here, with loyalty. **Station a garrison:** leave a legion or auxiliaries. **Raise forces:** auxiliary cohorts (fast) or a legion (months). Recruits come from this province and carry its group (for appeals to comrades) |
| **Governor** | Appoint from your friends and clients. Each has a trait (honest, greedy, able, timid) that changes revenue, unrest or loyalty. A capable man might not stay loyal |

**Pushback, recorded:** Crusader Kings and Total War campaigns last centuries
or decades; this one lasts three or four years. Buildings are therefore works
a governor could finish in a season (roads, granaries, harbours, a stone camp,
a mint), not monuments, and each must pay back within about a year of play or
it will never be built. Every building is a new system, so the measurement
tools must show each one being worth building somewhere, or it is cut.

## 7. Fixes for the kill gate's open issues

| # | Issue | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Military weakest on easier levels | The field gives the military road its edge: ruses, better generalship, absorbing broken legions. Re-measure after Phase 2 |
| 2 | Too few small fights off the military road | New skirmish types: river crossings, ambushes in the passes, rival detachments harassing a march, a town's sally, deserters. All fight-or-avoid |
| 3 | Conspiracy the top killer | **The whispers meter** on the Rome screen and top bar in Act IV, fed by legitimacy and Rome's unrest. An informer or a friend warns you a month ahead when a plot forms, and each road has a counter: purge (military), the Senate (politics), largesse (charisma), informers (wealth) |
| 4 | 14–19% stalemates | Rival AI: claimants who hold Rome send a second army out to break the weakest rival in reach; claimants who cannot take Rome make terms (fold into a stronger one) instead of waiting |
| 5 | Bypass decides nothing | Becomes **March past**, a route setting on the army: faster, but every body left behind cuts supply. Paired with a time pressure the bot and player feel: a rival marching on Rome shows its arrival month on the map |
| 6 | Politics background strong everywhere | Watch at playtest |

## 8. Saves

- Key: `imperator.save` (a contract, never renamed).
- Payload: `{ v, state }`, with a migration chain and fixtures from every
  version.
- Text export `IMPR1.` (deflated JSON, base64url) and a file download.
- Autosave at the start of every month; one manual slot.

## 9. Instruments

| Tool | Question | Must assert |
|---|---|---|
| `check` | Is anything impossible happening? | Kill-gate invariants, plus purse == ledger, and no building or order the UI offers that the engine ignores |
| `bot` | Can a full campaign be played? | Every run advances months and ends; no stalls |
| `measure:campaign` | Are the win rates still on target? | The kill-gate matrix, with the noise floor |
| `measure:battle` | Is the field tactical, not arithmetic? | Hand vs auto vs random targets; no pure-order policy wins; relief matters |
| `measure:options` | Is every encounter option and building worth using somewhere? | Ablations; an option or building that changes nothing gets cut |
| `smoke` | Does it work on a phone? | 390×844: every screen renders, no overflow, no raw tokens, reload keeps the save |

## 11. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Scope: a map game, a management game and a tactical game at once | The calibration phase settles one piece of each before the rest are built |
| The field could be tedious or solved | Measured targets set before tuning (`FRAME-BATTLE.md`); orders capped at six per sector; one-tap rounds |
| 41 regions on a 390-pixel screen | Pan and zoom; an empire list as the alternative to the map; big tap targets on armies |
| Buildings as decoration | Each must measure as worth building somewhere, or it is cut |
| Province management becoming chores | Defaults carry forward; batch changes from the empire list; at most a few decisions in a quiet month |

## 13. Phase 1, as built (2026-10-24)

Where this section and the plan above differ, this section wins.

**What exists**
- `src/engine/`: the kill-gate model split into 15 modules, plus diplomacy
  (`FRAME-DIPLOMACY.md`), the player's actions, `view()`, versioned saves and a
  money ledger.
- `src/data/map.js`: a 204×165 tile map built from Natural Earth by
  `tools/build-map.js`.
- `src/ui/`: the phone shell. It has a map with pan and zoom, plus province,
  provinces, army, diplomacy, Rome, news and legate screens. It also has
  encounter and offer cards, new game (difficulty, start, quick start or seven
  life choices, name), an end screen, and save codes (`IMPR1.`).
- PWA manifest, service worker, generated icons.

**Verified**
- **The split is faithful.** With diplomacy off and the old settings, the
  engine matches `sim/model.js` on 252 campaigns.
- **`tools/check.js`** checks its rules every turn over 168 campaigns,
  including purse = start + ledger.
- **`tools/bot.js`** plays campaigns through `act()` and `view()` only, with no
  stall or rejected action. A save reloaded at month 12 plays on identically.
- **`tools/smoke.mjs`** at 390×844: every tab renders with no overflow, raw
  tokens or console errors; 24 months played; end screen; save survives a
  reload.

**Found and fixed while building**
- In Act I rivals treated you as an open enemy, and an idle player lost his
  legion by month 10 in 84% of games. Now your legion counts as the Senate's
  until you take a province or are proclaimed. Beaten in Act I, it falls back to
  a neighbouring province instead of being destroyed.
- Dispatches were trace lines ("F11 rises at NIT"). Now they are written news,
  dated, and late from far provinces.

**Measured with diplomacy** (`tools/measure-campaign.js`, 140 runs per road per level)

| | Wealth | Military | Politics | Charisma | All | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Legionary | 44% | 29% | 54% | 45% | 43% | 35% |
| Centurion | 24% | 11% | 33% | 22% | 22% | 15% |
| Legate | 15% | 5% | 10% | 12% | 11% | 5% |

- **Diplomacy makes the game easier.** Its biggest lever, submission, was
  reined in (an office owed, trust first, one demand every three months).
- **Win rates are about 1.5× target.** Retuning waits for Phase 2, because
  buildings and the field will move these numbers again.
- **Military is the weakest road**, as at the kill gate.

**Known gaps carried to Phase 2**
- Buildings, governors and the tactical field.
- The whispers meter is shown but plots give no month's warning yet.
- Map borders need two hand corrections.
- Fonts load from Google, so the first offline load uses fallback faces.

## 14. Phase 2 (calibration), as built (2026-10-24)

Three pieces at full polish for the author's verdict, plus the fixes promised
in §7.

**1. The field** (`src/engine/battle.js`, `src/ui/field.js`, `docs/FRAME-BATTLE.md`)
- **One resolver** for every large battle: hand play, "leave it to the
  legates", AI against AI, and measurement.
- **Orders:** three sectors with six orders each, plus the reserve, cavalry on
  both wings, and the legate (ride, rally, appeal across the lines, ruse).
- **Line relief rotates lines.** The tired line passes back through the gaps
  and rests.
- **A spent line cannot brace**, and a broken front passes its panic to the
  line behind.
- **Four enemy generals**, who answer repetition. Their intent is never shown.
- **The screen is pixel art:** cohorts as blocks of soldier dots that thin as
  men fall, volleys, dust, relief and rout animated, night falling.
- **A calibration jump** on the title screen opens straight into Second
  Bedriacum.

Measured (`tools/measure-battle.js`, 300 battles a cell):

| Policy | Even 4 v 4 | Outnumbered 4 v 6 | Outnumbering 6 v 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Careful hand play | 58% | 26% | 74% |
| Leave it to the legates | 45% | 31% | 73% |
| Never relieve | 37% | 30% | 71% |
| Always hold | 19% | 13% | 74% |
| Always charge | 10% | 7% | 66% |
| Random | 5% | 3% | 94% |

**Open:**
- About a third of even battles end in a draw at night.
- Random orders do oddly well with superior numbers.

**2. Provinces** (`src/data/buildings.js`, `src/engine/buildings.js`)
- **Eight works**, each with a cost, months and upkeep.
- **Governors:** three named candidates per province with a trait; sometimes a
  friend from your past.
- **The sheet has four tabs:** overview (taxes, grain, games), buildings,
  forces and governor.
- **Every work matters somewhere.** `npm run measure:buildings` (sim/ablations/buildings.js) forbids each
  in turn: every one is built, and each changes 3–22 of 24 campaigns. The arms
  workshop was decoration and was folded into the levy office and the stone
  camp.

**3. The encounter card:** an emblem, the facts, and a line on what each
option risks.

**Fixes from §7 and from playing it**
- **Plot warnings.** Plots form a month before they strike. You may be warned;
  informers paid in time may uncover them. Conspiracy fell from 20–25% of
  endings to 3–4%.
- **Act I dead end.** A usurper could take your province while you plotted,
  leaving nothing to approach. Fixed, and the interface bot now fails if Act I
  ever has no way forward.
- **Map:** south-east Spain, La Mancha and the Balearics belong to
  Tarraconensis; the strip east of the Rhine mouth is outside the empire.
- **Snowball.** The player held 18 legions when Act IV opened. Now an army of
  more than eight legions in one place loses men each month (supply), and fewer
  beaten legions change sides.

**Balance** (`tools/measure-campaign.js`, 140 runs per road per level)

| | Wealth | Military | Politics | Charisma | All | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Legionary | 41% | 20% | 51% | 36% | 37% | 35% |
| Centurion | 19% | 6% | 21% | 13% | 15% | 15% |
| Legate | 4.3% | 3.6% | 2.1% | 8.6% | 4.6% | 5% |

- Every start on Legate wins 1.3–7.5%.
- **Open:**
  - Military still trails on the easier levels.
  - Politics is below and charisma above the 3–8% band on Legate.
  - Dying with your army is the top cause of loss (27–30%).

## 15. After the first playtest, as built (2026-10-26)

What the author hit on his phone, and what changed. The table of his words and
the class of each point is in `docs/FRAME-PLAYTEST1.md`.

**Money.** The first province was a trap: no income until it fell, then a
legion province's revenue (Germania Superior: 80k) against three legions' pay
(about 280k). Now:
- Taking a province from the Senate seizes its treasury: 1.5 months of revenue
  plus 180k for each legion stationed there by right (the pay chest). It refills
  over a year (`chestOf`, core.js). Germania Superior: 660k. Rivals get it too.
- A legion in its own province, held by its own side, costs 60% of its pay
  (requisitions in kind); two or more there add 1 unrest a month (`legionPay`,
  world.js).
- In Act I you draw a legate's salary, 15k a month.
- One function each for income, pay, upkeep and the monthly balance
  (`incomeOf`, `payOf`, `upkeepOf`, `balanceOf`); the HUD, the Provinces tab and
  the report all read them.

**Promises.** The promise option shows the month it falls due and your monthly
balance. A promise can be half-paid once: the rest waits three more months
(`payPart`).

**Encounters and retreat.** A rival army coming at yours no longer fights at
once: it halts and you see both sides (men, legions, who commands, the odds)
and choose **Stand and fight** or **Fall back** toward your nearest held
province (the rearguard loses 3%). Undecided at month's end: you fall back, as
with every other encounter. On the field, **Sound the retreat** ends the battle
as a loss without a rout: the lines in contact lose 10%, the rest 4%, no
legions go over, no death roll, and the army falls back (`soundRetreat`,
battle.js; `fallBack`, core.js). The AI's own decision to attack is unchanged:
it attacks only when it would have fought before.

**Armies and generals** (generals.js). Your legions no longer gather on their
own. Each legion is in an army or holds the province it stands in. Generals
swear to you when your province falls (one), when a rival submits (he keeps
his army, as your general), and in events; at most four. A general's army
marches on your orders, fights in his style (methodical, cautious, impetuous,
cunning), adds 4% strength per point of skill, and he can die when it is
beaten. You are only on the field with the army you lead: elsewhere your
legate actions are not offered. Actions: form an army, split one, join, leave a
legion as garrison, give an army to a general, disband. Legions sent to an army
elsewhere march as a column and merge on arrival. Only the human player has
generals; rivals and the measurement bot are unchanged.

**Explanation.** A one-screen report after every month (money in and out by
kind, provinces and legions won or lost, men, legitimacy, the news that reached
you, up to four warnings, the next step). A "Now" line on the map and Army tab
says what to do next and which tab does it. Short guides on the battle screen
and the province sheet. The province overview shows works and forces with a
button to each tab; the Provinces list has Build, Forces and Governor buttons.

**Story** (story.js). Three opening pages (the world in March 68, how the war is
won, your command), a page for each new act and for Nero's death, and 17
character events with two or three answers each, at most one a month (40%),
most once a campaign. Every answer moves a number: money through the ledger,
loyalty, legitimacy, unrest, suspicion, men, a general. Left undecided, the
last (quiet) answer is taken. Human games only, on their own random stream, so
measurement is untouched. A record of your answers is on the Legate tab.

**Balance after the changes** (25–40 campaigns per start and road): Legionary
39%, Centurion 15%, Legate 4.8% (Legate rival funds 2.6 → 2.3, since rivals
seize treasuries too). Battle: careful play 58% vs auto 42% in even battles,
unchanged.

**Open.** Military road weak on Legionary (25% against 41–47%); wealth weakest
on Legate (1.8%). The interface bot plays to the end without crashing but
rarely wins: it is a stall and crash test, not a player. The events are
untuned: none is large enough to swing a campaign, which is deliberate until
the author has played them.

## 16. Depth: the Senate, the frontiers, trade, rivals, story (2026-10-26)

The plan is `docs/FRAME-DEPTH.md`. Every system below plays by the same rules
for the rivals, and `npm run measure:depth` (sim/ablations/depth.js) plays the same campaigns with
each one switched off.

**The Senate** (senate.js). Favour 0–100 per claimant, drifting toward
15 + 0.6 × legitimacy (+10 emperor, +5 decree, −25 public enemy), 8% a month.
Court the senators (80k, +8, once a month). One motion a month: the decree
(legate of the Senate and People, legitimacy +8); proconsular imperium
(recognition at legitimacy 15 instead of 25); thanksgiving after a pitched
battle won (legitimacy +5); the treasury of Saturn (+300k, favour −15, every
six months, only holding Rome); restoring Nero's exiles (100k, favour +12,
once); declaring a rival a public enemy (holding Rome or the throne, favour 60
and 15 above his: his legitimacy −12, legions −6 loyalty and −0.5 a month,
unrest +8, no recognition for a year). Taking Rome lifts the decree against
you, a month late. An AI emperor with favour 55 outlaws his strongest
disliked rival (25% a month, once each). Favour moves the hold: plots ×0.8 at
60 or more, ×1.3 under 25. Plunder −2, broken treaty −4, recognition by
terror −20, a discovered incitement −10.

**Frontier peoples** (foreign.js). Brigantes, Batavians, Dacians and
Sarmatians, Parthia, Garamantes, each on one frontier's threat. Subsidy (60–150k):
six months at 35% of the threat's rise; needs a province on that frontier.
Incite (90–220k): threat +30 and ×1.8 for four months; a rebel within two
provinces; 35% found out (legitimacy −6, favour −10, the holders' standing
−30). Parthian friendship (220k, for whoever holds Syria): the East's threat
rises at 30%, luxuries, and the silk road (+25k a month). Rivals subsidise
frontiers past 70 and now and then incite against a stronger claimant.

**Trade** (trade.js). Six goods from the provinces that made them (data/powers.js).
Each good a province makes brings 6k a month to market (+30% with a harbour,
half above 60 unrest). Agreements with a rival at peace: 10k a month for each
good he has that you lack, and the good itself; war ends them. Effects of
holding a good: metal (levies 20% cheaper, +0.05 drill), horses (cavalry
+25%, small fights +4%), timber (works 20% cheaper), grain (winter crossings
and enemies left behind cost half), wine and oil (legion loyalty +0.3 a
month), luxuries (legitimacy +0.3 a month, games 25% cheaper).

**Rivals.** Proposals: trade, a truce with gold (+0.1 per 100k, times his
personality), a province demanded from a partner at peace who is less than
half your strength. Personalities from Tacitus: Galba (gifts at half), Otho
(truces easier), Vitellius (gold weighs 1.5×), Vespasian (truces easy,
alliances hard).

**Story.** Twenty-one more events, including chains (the astrologer's
prophecy, the father-in-law, the priests' curse, the tribune who went over,
Civilis turning on Rome, your brother in Rome), the Senate (an accuser, an
embassy with the Vestals, the consulship, the Arval Brothers), trade
(bankers, Anicetus' pirates, a mine revolt, the merchants' tolls), the
frontiers (Civilis, Vologases' forty thousand horse, Cartimandua, the
Dacians), and rivals (an offer of retirement, which can end the campaign; a
legate for sale). Pages when an emperor is recognised and when one of the
four dies.

**Measured** (25 campaigns per start and road): Legionary 38%, Centurion 16%,
Legate 4.7%, on target. Ablation at Centurion (10 per start and road): all
systems 14.6%; without the Senate 13.2% (no motions, no outlawings: rivals
lose an average 0.6 outlawings of you per campaign, you lose imperium and the
decree); without frontier peoples 15.7% (breaches 4.3 → 5.2 a campaign, no
incitements); without trade 14.6% (same rate, different campaigns). Every
system changes individual campaigns; because rivals use them too, none moves
the overall rate much. First tuning casualty: grain originally halved the
supply-limit losses and pushed Centurion to 28%; it now protects winter
crossings instead.

**Open.** Road spread widened: on Centurion politics 27% and wealth 21%
against military 6% and charisma 10% (the Senate and markets reward the first
two). The measurement bot uses the Senate's honours but never outlaws, incites
or demands provinces, so those levers are untested by it; they need the author.

**Fixed on the way.** Act I could leave you with nothing to approach for
months when every other legion stood aside below three-quarters of the men;
legions standing aside no longer defend the governor.

## 17. Review, levers and the roads (2026-10-26)

**Code review of the depth systems** found eight bugs, all fixed and each
checked by a targeted test: a public enemy was recognised the same month the
decree lapsed; rivals could never restore the exiles; rivals with no hostile
neighbour never proposed trade; the AI emperor outlawed by a looser private
rule; trade outlived a broken truce for a month; client legions forgot their
king (and the Parthian horse could be taken twice); negative gold in a
proposal paid the proposer; the Rome tab showed the wrong price for games.
Not changed: the goods lookups repeat work, but a full screen renders in
1.5 ms, and caching them risked a reloaded save playing differently.

**Levers the measurement bot now uses**: sounding the retreat (when losing
worse than 0.6 from the third round), inciting a people against a stronger
rival, a truce with gold, demanding a province from a weak partner. Measured
on 560 fresh campaigns each at Centurion: the retreat cuts death in battle
from 34% to 25% of campaigns; gold for a truce is worth about a point;
demanding provinces is neutral; inciting indiscriminately costs about three
points (mostly the wealth road's money). Rule change: you cannot incite a
people against a frontier you hold part of (the bot had been burning its own
provinces: 5.4 breaches a campaign against 3.3).

**Measurement noise.** One extra random draw reshuffles a whole campaign, so
two variants on the same seeds are effectively independent samples. A first
lever ablation showed a nine-point "cost" for inciting that vanished on a
fresh seed set. Rule from now on: a lever's value is claimed only when it
holds on two seed sets.

**Roads.** Centurion before: politics 27%, wealth 21%, charisma 10%,
military 6%. Diagnosis: after taking Rome, every road mostly loses it to a
rival retaking the city, which is a matter of army size, and charisma and
military reached Rome late (first capture at months 24 and 31 against 17–22
for winners) because they grew slowly. Changes, each matching the road's
promise in the opening pages:
- the bot courts the Senate on every road (it had only on politics and wealth);
- booty: a pitched battle's winner takes 15% of the loser's treasury (30% on
  the military road);
- military: beaten legions join a military victor more often (+30%), and
  levies cost a quarter less; a commander's military skill lowers his risk of
  dying when beaten;
- charisma: wavering rival legions (loyalty under 45, not under their
  general's eye) next to a charismatic commander's army may come over on their
  own (2.5% a month per point of charisma); appeals gain 2% per point;
- every road's emperor keeps his legitimacy up to the same level (65); the
  military road by a triumph (100k, legitimacy +2, legions in Italy +3).
Tried and reverted: making the military bot more careful (1.15 → 1.3 odds;
kept, no effect), letting it talk first (+3, noise), counting the general's
skill in its readiness for Rome (reached Rome more often, held it no better).

**Measured after** (25 per start and road): Legionary 35% (hold 12 → 14
months), Centurion 15%, Legate 5.0%. Centurion by road: wealth 12, military
11, politics 22, charisma 14 on the standard seeds; 21, 9, 20, 19 on a second
set. Open: military still trails on two seed sets out of two; its problem is
holding Rome with a smaller army, which wants a design answer (for example
Vitellius' remedy: replacing the Praetorians with one's own legionaries)
rather than more tuning.

## 18. The guide, the field, a second ending, sound (2026-10-26)

**The guide** (tutorial.js): optional at a new campaign, on by default. Eight
steps, Army tab to the Senate; each done when the game shows it was done (an
approach made, a province held, a work begun, an army marching, the Senate page
opened), with a button to the tab and a pulsing nav icon. Kept in the save;
skippable. The interface bot walks a guided campaign to its end.

**The field.** Words over each sector for what just happened (CHARGE, THEY
CHARGE, PILA, FRESH LINE, RESERVE, HORSE ON THE FLANK, YOUR LINE BREAKS, THEY
BREAK); sparks where a charge lands; a red wash and men streaming away where a
line breaks; VICTORY, DEFEAT or RETREAT across the field at the end, and the
men each side lost. "Leave it to the general" now stops on the result too.

**A second ending: two Augusti** (diplomacy.js). In the last act, when you and
one rival hold two-thirds of all claimants' legions, have been at peace six
months, and neither is twice the other's strength, you may propose dividing the
empire. He weighs it like any proposal (more willing in a stalemate, less on
harder levels). Accepted, the campaign ends in a lesser victory. The
measurement bot proposes it whenever it may. Measured: 2.3% of Legionary
campaigns, 0.9% of Centurion, 0.4% of Legate end this way.

**Sound** (sound.js): synthesised in the browser, no files. Trumpets for a
round, drums at the month's end, a fanfare or a low horn for the result, a
chime when a decision waits. On by default; a switch on the Legate tab,
remembered per device.

**Balance after all of it** (25 per start and road): Legionary 33.6% (hold
14 → 15 months), Centurion 15.6%, Legate 5.3%.

## 19. Your circle: governors, generals, and men who turn (2026-10-27)

The author: "generals and governors should pull from the same pool of
characters ... given a salary. The governors will then automatically manage
their provinces based off of their trait and your standing instructions.
Generals can do that too." And, on the proposal that your own men can rebel
and that generals settle small fights but ask before large ones: "I agree,
continue on".

**One circle** (`src/engine/circle.js`), at most 8 men. Each has Governing and
Command (1–4), a trait (able, honest, greedy, ambitious, timid), a battle
style (impetuous, cunning, methodical, cautious), loyalty and ambition. Men
come from your provinces (local men), your past (friends), rivals who submit,
and the legates of provinces you take. Every man is paid each month with the
legions: 3k + 1.5k per point of his better skill (+2k if honest); staff half,
governors + revenue/50, generals + 1k per legion (to 6). Unpaid, loyalty falls
6 a month.

**Governors** (`govern.js`) run their province each month on a standing
instruction: balance, exploit, pacify, fortify, develop, or manual. Trait
bends it: a timid man will not tax heavily, a greedy one will not tax lightly
and keeps 10% of the revenue. They build from a list for the instruction,
within the realm's works budget (0/40/80/150k a month), keeping two months'
pay in reserve. Rivals hire and instruct governors the same way, by road.

**Generals** (`orders.js`) carry out an order each month: hold, guard the
frontier, expand, hunt, support you, or manual. They settle small encounters
by their style (each needs different odds to attack; a defender stands at
three quarters of that). Before a pitched battle above your threshold (10k,
18k, 30k men, or never) they ask you. On expand, a general leaves his weakest
legion on a threatened frontier he would otherwise strip.

**Loyalty and ambition.** Loyalty drifts toward 45 + (legitimacy − 40)/2,
+15 for an old friend, +20 for family by marriage, +10 on the military road
(a soldier's officers are his own men). A man more than three provinces from
your army loses half a point a month. A general's victories add ambition (+1
a small fight, +4 a large one, +6 a pitched battle).

**Danger** = (ambition − loyalty)/100 × min(1, legions he holds / 4) × 1.5 if
your legitimacy is under 40 × difficulty × 0.3. A son held as hostage makes it
0. At 1.5% a month he is **restless**: you are told, the Court card shows it,
and the warnings list it. From the next month he may act at that chance. Before
July 69 he proclaims himself with his legions (and his province, if he
governs one); after it, he goes over to the strongest claimant. Governors
without legions may still take an unguarded province when ambition runs 15
past loyalty and you look weak.

**Counters**: a gift (50k, +10 loyalty, not more than once in three months);
a marriage tie (two at most: +15 loyalty, −10 ambition, +20 to his loyalty
target); his son as hostage (no rebellion; −5 loyalty). Relieving a restless
general with four or more legions provokes him three times in ten.

**The same rules for rivals.** Their men turn too, and they answer with gifts
when they can afford them. Difficulty scales the player's danger (0.5, 1,
1.3) and the rivals' (1, 0.8, 0.45): on Legate your rivals keep their men
better than you do.

**Balance after the three rounds** (start pool, 4 roads): Legionary 34.6–35.7%,
Centurion 14.3–16.3%, Legate 4.8–4.9% (Legate hold 24 → 27 months). On the
author's Act IV save with legitimacy forced to 15, rebellions came about 0.85
per ten months with no answer, and none when restless men were honoured.
